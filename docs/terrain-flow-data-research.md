# Terrain and water-flow data for Viana do Castelo

Research date: 15 September 2026. Sources below are first-party catalogues, services, or documentation.

## Ranked recommendation

1. **Production: DGT 2 m bare-earth MDT + APA/SNIAmb directed hydrography.** DGT's 2024–2025 LiDAR programme covers mainland Portugal and publishes 0.5 m, 2 m, and 10 m GeoTIFF terrain models. Use 2 m as the practical master raster around Viana do Castelo; derive hillshade, contours, slope, depression-filled D8 flow direction, and flow accumulation offline. Overlay APA's geocoded river network as the authoritative visible drainage layer. Its schema includes `flowdirection`, `startnode`, `endnode`, `from_node`, `to_node`, and `nextdownid`, so animated downstream strokes can use supplied network direction rather than guessing from line order. [DGT terrain models](https://www.dgterritorio.gov.pt/atividades/cartografia/cartografia-topografica/modelos-digitais) · [DGT download catalogue](https://cdd.dgterritorio.gov.pt/dgt-fe/catalogos) · [APA river layer schema](https://sniambgeoogc.apambiente.pt/getogc/rest/services/SNIAmb/RdHidrografica_GeoCodificada/MapServer/0)
2. **Fast prototype: OpenTopography Continental Europe DTM 30 m, cross-checked with APA.** It is bare-earth rather than a surface model and can be requested for a small bounding box through the Global Datasets API. It is quick enough for testing terrain tint, hillshade, contours, and generalized flow paths, but 30 m cells cannot resolve streets, culverts, or parcel-scale drainage. [OpenTopography Europe DTM](https://opentopography.org/news/new-30m-digital-terrain-model-covering-continental-europe-now-available) · [developer/API documentation](https://opentopography.org/developers)
3. **Portable fallback: Copernicus DEM GLO-30.** It has global 30 m coverage, GeoTIFF delivery, and a free licence with mandatory attribution. It is a **digital surface model** containing buildings and vegetation, so it is attractive for hillshade but can create false barriers in urban flow modelling. Current direct access through Copernicus Data Space requires registration for Copernicus Contributing Missions data; OpenTopography also serves the 2023_1 version by API and cloud-optimized GeoTIFF. [Copernicus product page](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/copernicus-contributing-missions/collections-description/COP-DEM) · [Copernicus API documentation](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/APIs/SentinelHub/Data/DEM.html) · [OpenTopography distribution note](https://opentopography.org/news/updated-copernicus-30m-DEM-available)

HydroSHEDS/HydroRIVERS are useful as a broad regional sanity check, not as the principal visual source at city scale.

## What the prototype should communicate

The red source layer is a selection/state layer, not evidence of direction. A terrain visualization should add three different kinds of evidence, each labelled honestly:

- **Terrain:** a quiet hypsometric tint (high land warm/light, low land cool/dark) combined with multidirectional hillshade and sparse labelled contours. This lets users perceive the valley without reading arrows.
- **Likely surface-flow paths:** preprocess the bare-earth DEM by filling/breaching depressions, then compute D8 direction and accumulation. Render only higher-accumulation paths as thin translucent blue threads. Label them “terrain-derived likely flow”; they are model output, not mapped drains.
- **Known waterways:** draw APA lines above the derived threads and animate a subtle dash/particle in the service's recorded `flowdirection`. Label these “mapped watercourses.” Use basin polygons only on hover/selection, with low opacity.

For the eventual runoff-contribution gradient shown in the reference image, elevation alone is insufficient. Flow accumulation says how much land drains through a cell, not how much water runs off. A defensible runoff layer also needs rainfall and loss factors such as imperviousness, soil/infiltration, and drainage infrastructure. Until those inputs exist, call the display **terrain-derived drainage potential** rather than runoff volume.

## Practical pipeline

1. Download the DGT 2 m MDT tiles for the selected catchment and mosaic/reproject them in PT-TM06/ETRS89.
2. Hydrologically condition the mosaic: remove small artifacts, fill or breach sinks, and burn in verified APA watercourses. Add known culverts/storm drains if the product needs street-scale claims.
3. Generate static raster/vector assets offline: elevation tint, multidirectional hillshade, 5–10 m contours, slope, D8 direction, and log-scaled accumulation. Clip and simplify APA river/basin features.
4. Publish map-ready tiles (terrain/hillshade as COG or raster tiles; contours and flow paths as vector tiles). Keep source dates, attribution, processing version, and confidence in metadata.
5. In the browser, composite muted terrain first, selected source area second, derived flow threads third, and authoritative APA watercourses last. Animate only a sparse subset and respect reduced-motion settings.

This pipeline keeps large DEM processing and third-party credentials out of the client, makes the map fast, and separates observed data from inferred water movement.
