WaterScope Design Direction

Visual direction · for review

WaterScope, by the community.

The agreed direction for colors, fonts, type sizes and status shapes for the WaterScope web platform in Viana do Castelo. The goal is an app that feels made for this place and these people, not a generic AI template. Layout decisions are out of scope here except for the open questions at the end.

Audience: residents aged 14 to 85+ with private wells, springs, mines and reservoirsFraming: citizen science, community action

The vibe

A neighbourhood notice board: warm and approachable in its shapes, bold and collective in its voice.

Warm shapes

Cream paper background, soft white cards with large rounded corners, pill buttons. Few hard lines, no heavy shadows.

Bold, collective voice

Chunky headlines and a "we" tone: Our water, measured by us. Community numbers like "34 sources tested" are shown with pride.

Local roots

Azulejo-inspired blue, granite ink, and the cream of the paper test strips. Place names are shown in blue so Afife stands apart from the source name.

Color

All text colors meet WCAG AA (4.5:1) on their backgrounds. Shapes and map marks meet 3:1.

Status system

Three meanings, each with its own shape, color and word, so no one has to rely on color alone. Readings are two-state (inside or outside the safe limit), matching the app's existing logic. Hazards are reports from people, not test results, so they get the warning triangle.

Chips: pill shape, 14px bold, colored text, background tint mixed 10% into papel, plus a 1px edge in the fill color at 35% opacity so the chip stays visible on cream.

On the map Pin shape shows the reading. A small gold triangle on the corner means a hazard was reported. (See open decision 2.)

Reading scale

The existing strip-scale picker stays as designed: grey track, "Safe limit" bracket, values at their true positions, selected value highlighted. The dot stays two-state: green inside the safe limit, red outside. Only the palette changes.

Fonts

Two families, each with one job. Both are free on Google Fonts and support Portuguese characters (ã, ç, õ).

Bricolage Grotesque · ExtraBold 800

Our water, measured by us.

Headings and the single hero number on a result screen. Never for buttons, chips or data in lists.

Atkinson Hyperlegible · Regular 400, Bold 700

Don't use it in baby bottles. For adults, no immediate risk.

0123456789 · Add reading

Everything else: body text, readings and values, labels, buttons, chips, legend. Designed by the Braille Institute for low-vision readers, with digits that are hard to confuse.

Shapes

Always drawn as icons on a 16 × 16 grid, never typed characters (fonts draw ● ▲ ◆ at different sizes). The diamond and triangle are slightly larger than the circle so all three look equal.

Sizes: 14, 16 or 18px next to text; 20–24px on the map with a 2.5px white outline. Always pair a shape with its word.

Accessibility rules

  • Never color alone.Every status has a shape and a word.
  • Contrast.4.5:1 minimum for text, 3:1 for icons and borders. The palette above already meets this.
  • Size up.17px body text, 48px minimum touch targets, and a Large text option for older users.
  • Readable outdoors.Dark ink on warm paper, no pale grey text, no thin weights.
  • Plain language.Verdicts say what to do ("Don't use it in baby bottles"), not just a number.
  • Room for Portuguese.Portuguese labels run 20–30% longer than English. Buttons and chips must allow for it.

Open decisions

These change layout or how data is shown, so they're left for review together.

1 · Source detail sheet: rows or tiles?

List rows currentOne reading per row: measure, value, status. Calm, table-like, scales well when more tests (E. coli) are added.
TilesReadings in small side-by-side cards. More visual at a glance, gets crowded beyond two measures.

2 · What should map pins show?

Hazard only currentPin color only says whether a hazard was reported (green = none, red = reported). Test results are not shown on the map.
Reading + hazard badgePin shape shows the reading (circle or diamond); a small gold triangle marks a reported hazard. Avoids red meaning two different things.

3 · Catchment colors

Purple and orange currentThe orange is close to the hazard gold and could be confused with it.
Shades of blueWatersheds read as water. Only one catchment is highlighted at a time, so blue variants are enough.

4 · Language

EnglishCurrent interface text.
Portuguese or bilingualBetter fit for residents. Needs design with longer labels in mind.

Note: the reading data (qualityBands.js) also labels bands as "caution" or "concern" with verdict sentences. The interface does not show a separate caution level, and this direction keeps it two-state. The triangle is reserved for hazards.
