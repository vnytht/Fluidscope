# Elevation-based source connectivity: precedents and method

Research checked 20 September 2026. Here, a *source* may mean an incident/release location or a downstream asset such as a water intake. Elevation can establish a plausible surface-water connection; it does not by itself establish contamination, exposure, timing, or concentration.

## Established precedents

| Project | What it demonstrates | Relevance to Fluidscope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [USGS StreamStats](https://www.usgs.gov/national-hydrography/applications-streamstats) | Delineates the land draining to a selected stream point, traces a raindrop from land to the network, and navigates upstream/downstream to indexed features. Its [navigation services](https://www.usgs.gov/streamstats/web-services) expose flow path, path between two network locations, and upstream/downstream feature searches. | Closest precedent for the combined land-to-stream and stream-to-asset interaction. It is a US data service, not a Portugal dataset. |
| [EPA NHDPlus tools](https://www.epa.gov/waterdata/nhdplus-tools) | The Flow Table Navigator walks a directed network upstream with tributaries or downstream with divergences. | Clear precedent for directed graph traversal after the release enters a mapped stream. US only. |
| [EPA How's My Waterway](https://mywaterway.epa.gov/community) | Displays an upstream watershed around a selected watershed and relates upstream activities to downstream waters. | Clear UX precedent for showing contributing land separately from receiving waters. |
| [GRASS GIS `r.watershed`](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/r.watershed.html) + [`r.water.outlet`](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/r.water.outlet.html) | Creates flow direction, flow accumulation and streams from an elevation raster, then finds cells upstream of a chosen outlet. | Reproducible local processing pipeline for a Portugal DEM. `r.water.outlet` is an *upstream* basin query; downstream tracing is a separate operation. |
| [HydroSHEDS](https://www.hydrosheds.org/products) / [HydroBASINS](https://www.hydrosheds.org/products/hydrobasins) / [HydroRIVERS](https://www.hydrosheds.org/products/hydrorivers) | Publishes conditioned elevation, flow-direction/accumulation grids, nested catchments and connected river reaches globally. | Useful prototype or fallback network. [HydroSHEDS cautions](https://www.hydrosheds.org/faq) that local small-catchment errors can be substantial. |

## Calculation for a selected release point

1. Start with a **bare-earth DEM** covering the point, its likely basin, and a margin downstream. Match its projection/resolution to the hydrography. Correct artificial pits and reconcile the DEM with verified river lines, culverts, storm drains, and coastal outlets. The [USGS StreamStats technical history](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1514/full) describes direction and accumulation grids and how coastal flats and closed drainage complicate this work.
2. Compute a flow-direction grid and flow-accumulation grid. Accumulation marks likely concentrated channels; it is *contributing area*, not measured discharge or pollutant amount. The [GRASS workflow](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/r.watershed.html) provides both grids and a stream layer.
3. From an off-stream release, follow each cell's downhill direction until it reaches a verified waterway, sink, storm drain, or coast. Record the entry point and uncertainty. From an on-stream release, snap only to a hydrologically plausible nearby reach; display the original and snapped positions. [USGS notes](https://www.usgs.gov/streamstats/common-user-questions) that placing a point off the stream grid can yield a misleadingly tiny basin.
4. Traverse the **directed waterway graph downstream** from the entry reach. Any registered intake or monitored site on a reachable downstream reach is *potentially connected*. Traverse upstream from a selected intake to find possible contributing releases. Preserve branches at confluences/divergences. StreamStats and EPA demonstrate these operations in the links above.
5. If an asset is off-stream, first identify its intake/outfall/connection point. Merely falling inside a watershed polygon is insufficient to establish a connection. If the user asks which areas feed an intake, delineate the *upstream* basin at that intake (`r.water.outlet`); if they ask which assets a release may reach, trace *downstream* from its entry point.
6. Map the result as: selected release, downhill overland path, stream-entry point, directed downstream reaches, potentially connected assets, and an uncertainty layer for unmodeled infrastructure or ambiguous outlets. Show elevation tint or hillshade as explanatory context, not as the proof of an affected source.

## Interpretation and limits

- Label results **“potential downstream connection”**, not “affected” or “contaminated.” Connectivity does not quantify dilution, travel time, retention, deposition, chemical behavior, rainfall, or actual exposure. For those questions, use a calibrated flow and water-quality/transport model plus measurements; [EPA BASINS](https://www.epa.gov/hydrowq/better-assessment-science-integrating-point-and-non-point-sources-basins) is a precedent for combining watershed data and such models.
- Urban runoff can bypass terrain through underground infrastructure. [USGS shows](https://www.usgs.gov/streamstats/news/storm-drain-functionality) different contributing areas when storm drains and culverts are enforced in the DEM. At Fluidscope's coastal sites, flat terrain and tidal/coastal processes also weaken a simple downhill assumption; [USGS documents](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1514/full) coastline-specific corrections and common delineation errors.
- A smooth blue gradient can display accumulation or distance-to-outlet, but a large colored area must not imply that all of it is reached *from* the selected source. The upstream contributing area and downstream receiving network are opposite directions.

## Recommended prototype

Use APA's Portugal river network as the basis for an authoritative downstream graph, and DEM flow direction for the short land-to-water link and terrain context. The current `public/data/apa_rivers_viana.geojson` is a clipped display layer, not yet a validated routing graph: `GeoJSON/scripts/get_apa_rivers.py` preserves `next_down_id` but drops node and flow-direction fields, and its bounding-box clip can cut links at the edge. Rebuild and validate topology before using it to classify pins. Display **three distinct geometries**: pale upstream drainage area when examining an intake, a narrow downhill path from a land release, and bright downstream connected reaches. Add asset pins only when their hydrological connection points lie on those reaches. Defer any claim of impact magnitude until flow and pollutant data are available.
