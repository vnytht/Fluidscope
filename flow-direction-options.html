WaterScope concept lab

Make the watershed feel alive.

Three ways to show where surface water moves after someone selects a source. Each keeps the current catchment meaning while making direction easier to see on a phone.

The design constraint

Fractured granite can react quickly after rain, but the map cannot claim an exact underground path. These concepts show landscape-scale surface drainage and say so plainly.

01 Organic network Recommended

Living Current

Soft light traces travel along the river network like glints on moving water. The flow is immediately visible without covering the map in arrow icons.

  • Particles follow mapped river lines, with fewer traces on minor tributaries.
  • A bright head and fading tail make direction readable at a glance.
  • Motion pauses during pan and zoom; static chevrons replace it for reduced motion.

02 Basin sequence

Catchment Relay

A broad pulse hands water from one catchment to the next, then dissolves at the mapped outlet. It turns the data’s trusted basin sequence into a short visual event.

  • One slow 4–6 second playback avoids a distracting permanent loop.
  • Numbered boundary nodes keep direction clear when motion is paused.
  • The wide schematic ribbon avoids claiming an exact river or groundwater route.

03 Comprehension first

Water Journey

Three numbered landmarks turn hydrology into a route people can retell: this source, a receiving stream, and a recognizable downstream destination.

  • The map and bottom sheet repeat the same 1 → 2 → 3 sequence.
  • Direction remains fully readable without animation or color.
  • Local place names make a technical catchment model meaningful to residents.
